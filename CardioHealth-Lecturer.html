    What this is

    CardioHealth estimates the chance that a person has diabetes. It offers two checks, each built on a different real dataset:

    • Lifestyle check. Fourteen answers a person can give without any test: age, sex, BMI, blood pressure and cholesterol history, heart disease, stroke, walking difficulty, general health, and habits such as exercise, smoking, fruit and vegetables.
    • Clinical check. Age, BMI, blood pressure and, mainly, a 2-hour glucose test result.

    Both run entirely inside your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

    The four risk bands

    The model gives a percentage. The screening cut-off is the lowest percentage that still catches at least 80% of real cases in training. The bands are measured against that cut-off: Low is under half of it, Medium is half to 80% of it, Borderline is 80% of it up to the cut-off, and High is at or above it. These band edges are a design choice, not a clinical standard.

    Limits you should know

    • The lifestyle model learned from US adults in 2015 who answered a phone survey. The clinical model learned from 768 women of one community. Neither has been tested on Nigerian or other populations.
    • Survey answers are self-reported, and the data shows association, not cause.
    • The percentages reflect diabetes rates in those datasets. In another population the true rates would differ.
    • It screens for diabetes. It does not measure heart disease.
    • It cannot replace a laboratory test or a clinician.

    Data sources

    Lifestyle: CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System 2015, in the cleaned "Diabetes Health Indicators" form. Clinical: Pima Indians Diabetes Database, US National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases.
